---
name: develop-system-concept
description: Help a systems engineer investigate or develop a system concept by connecting the problem, desired outcomes, functions, alternatives, and feasibility evidence. Scenario-only and overall CONOPS work have separate skills.
---

Assist the engineer with the current concept question at the depth needed now. Start from existing work. Framing a need, investigating uncertainty, or comparing one aspect of alternatives can be the whole task; a complete proposal is optional.

For changes with several dependent outputs, difficult check failures, or review handoffs, read [planning, diagnosis and review](references/work-review.md) and apply the relevant section. Keep small edits and discussion direct. The user's explicit instructions take precedence over skill guidance. Preserve failed findings during repair and reconcile delivered artifacts and current check evidence with the requested scope before claiming completion.

The engineer directs scope, interpretation, priorities, accepted assumptions, tradeoffs, and selection. Prepare evidence, calculations, alternatives, and recommendations. Preserve settled decisions and differing stakeholder perspectives. Elicit missing intent with targeted questions or concrete examples when it affects the current decision. Continue independent work with explicit unknowns; routine edits and checks need no confirmation. Inferred needs, new requirements, and thresholds remain proposals with their basis; weights require actual preferences.

Use these activities as relevant to the current question and maturity:

- Investigate the present gap and desired outcome/timeframe. Use supplied notes with their linked passages; select existing evidence, focused elicitation, observation, or a rough prototype to resolve a particular uncertainty. Prepare external investigation for the engineer unless execution is authorized. Preserve requested prototype fidelity.
- Distinguish needs from proposed products while retaining explicit user constraints. Clarify terms, responsibilities, boundary, and essential functions before committing to components. Link functions to needs; coarse components, interfaces, and allocations are optional aids to assessment.
- Explore meaningful alternative approaches and compare them against stakeholder objectives and constraints, including people, integration, lifecycle effort, and uncertainty where relevant. Keep qualitative criteria. Use quantitative trade studies when inputs and preferences support them; no fixed alternative count is required.
- Test feasibility claims with appropriate calculations, evidence, prototypes, or simulations. Plan evaluation data, environments, and stakeholder feedback. Surface unsupported assumptions and conflicting constraints. Modification, further inquiry, and no selection remain possible outcomes.

- Incorporate feedback into affected earlier work. Return the useful change, material findings, and any next engineer decision. Stop at the requested boundary; omit filler and repeated questionnaires.

For a selected workload/capacity question, reuse the [conditional resource inquiry](../../docs/inquiry-profile.md) through `cse.py question answer`; preserve native identities and supply sources explicitly. State the availability hypothesis, units, capability/rate/composition assumptions and any shared-resource constraints. Compare alternatives without automatic selection or invented stakeholder weights. Do not derive rates from proposed task durations or treat a passing count/rate calculation as staffing or operational qualification. Apply Little's Law only through its explicit conditional profile; missing applicability stays unknown.

When human work or human/automation allocation matters, use the [human-task workflow](../../docs/human-task-review.md). Link tasks to the same model's functions/scenarios and explicit performer roles; select needed conditions, skills, equipment, procedures, timing provenance, concurrency, interruption/recovery, training and evaluation facts in the project scope. Reuse the normal concept-review command. Keep proposed facts, reported observations and unknowns distinct. Assignment counts, plausible prose and proposed durations do not establish competence, capacity, staffing adequacy or effective automation. Do not choose human/automation allocation from a generic superiority list or add rejected task-network/fatigue methods.

For recorded concept development, use the [project-tailored review procedure](references/concept-review.md) as part of the normal drafting loop. Start from the project's existing scope or prepare a small proposed scope from its question, sources and relevant operational/lifecycle situations. Select the people, technology, facilities, procedures, support, interfaces and evaluation preparations that actually matter; explain exclusions and retain unresolved applicability. Keep that scope separate from the candidate so deleting an expected function, responsibility or evaluation plan remains detectable. Run `cse.py concept-review check` against the retained scope revision after relevant model edits. Handle the files and commands for the engineer. A discussion alone needs no model or scope file, and incomplete drafting may continue with explicit unknowns. Do not generate obligations from whatever happens to remain in the candidate, silently narrow the scope to obtain a pass, or impose universal assignment counts. Consequential changes to accepted intent or applicability remain engineer decisions.

When independent organizations or shared interfaces matter, use the [ownership review](../../docs/ownership-review.md) in that same tailored scope. Preserve selected owner/operator/funder/change/support/acceptance parties and agreement, change or withdrawal facts. Shared roles may be appropriate. A proposed responsibility or technical connection does not authenticate consent, funding or continuity; ordinary drafting does not require a formal approval board.

Reuse linked operational artifacts. Use available `$develop-operational-scenarios` or `$develop-conops` only when that work is needed. Concept exploration does not require a complete CONOPS. When multiple engineering skills are requested, apply those skills to one shared SysML model or explicitly imported files with common definitions and IDs. Run the draft-shape checks for each requested skill; concept plus scenario work does not require a full CONOPS. Accept natural skill names and handle files, SysML syntax, and commands for the engineer.

Read supplied raw material in place. Keep raw source and private annotations out of Git and out of generated model/test reports; retain concise derived statements and source references needed for review. Keep user-specific models and reports in the selected project's ignored/private work directory (this repository: `work/` on `/workspace`), outside an installed plugin's directory, and inspect Git status before any commit. If raw material is already tracked, surface that fact instead of silently copying or publishing it.

Computed checks require a local execution environment. The shared SysML runtime is qualified on Linux with Python 3.10+ and Java/javac 21+; native Windows SysML is unsupported. When local execution is unavailable, continue useful qualitative work and identify checks as not run. For an installed plugin, set `CSE_SYSML_CACHE` to a private writable cache on the user's workspace before parser setup/checking; keep that value consistent across commands.

Establish function and view quality proportionately to the requested task. Functions express actions or transformations; recommend verb-object names, distinguish them from component and actor names, and assess meaning with the engineer rather than a naive verb dictionary. A function tree expresses decomposition, an activity diagram control/object flow, and a sequence diagram ordered participant exchanges; none substitutes for another. For a requested hierarchy, establish an explicit level-0/root, numbered decomposition levels, and parent-child traceability. Check requested levels, orphans, cycles, and duplicate IDs. Tailor breadth, depth, counts, and completeness to the brief; no fixed counts apply universally. Sequences need identified participants and meaningful ordered exchanges; add numeric min <= max timing ranges with units and a timing basis when the task requires them. Keep unsupported timing unresolved. Preserve justified exceptions explicitly and consequential changes as proposals. Use the optional documentary view checks described in [the SysML record guidance](references/sysml.md); distinguish machine-verifiable structure from human assessment of action meaning, adequacy, and completeness.

When the selected work involves user-facing interaction or an operational handoff, inspect relevant current professional/UI precedents, then use editable connected low-fidelity wireframes to expose assumptions. Show a small concrete walkthrough early, make fields/options/defaults/actions/states and recovery explicit, and keep engineering mappings adjacent. Iterate with user feedback before substantial expansion, polish or implementation. Follow [the shared interaction-walkthrough workflow](../../docs/interaction-walkthroughs.md) for source/model/control/requirement coverage and evidence limits; preserve existing authorization and independently invocable scope.

For new recorded work, maintain a SysML v2 `.sysml` model as the authoritative concept artifact; respect discussion-only requests. Read [the SysML record guidance](references/sysml.md) and use [the validated synthetic template](templates/default.sysml) when useful. Preserve qualitative unknowns and link operational elements rather than copying them. Run `python3 <skill-dir>/scripts/cse.py check <model>.sysml --syntax-only --kind concept` after edits; this launcher resolves the shared engine regardless of the current directory. Compile designated CSE clauses with its `check` command and run `--review` only when independent scope and supplied evidence exist. Ordinary documentation is not executable.

For behavior or feasibility claims, select a check using the [supported analysis correspondence](../../docs/behavior-review.md). Match the exact question, native identity, independent scope, evidence and engine semantics. Documentary entry/completion facts, parsed native behavior and sampled observations support different claims; unsupported native execution remains unresolved.

When a supported sampled requirement is present, run `python3 <skill-dir>/scripts/cse.py testgen <model>.sysml` as part of local draft evaluation and show the synthetic pass/fail variable-influence pairs. If none is present, say what remains qualitative or unsupported without inventing a threshold. Report uncovered variables and search limits; generated tests are not observed behavior evidence or FLIP coverage.

Return a concise engineer-facing concept view, links to the saved model and requested operational views, and the actual check findings. Explain assumptions, proposals, and unresolved questions in plain language. Distinguish parser/shape acceptance, controlled-clause compilation, synthetic test coverage, and checks against supplied behavior; avoid a single unqualified “verified” claim. Derive documents and useful diagrams from the shared model, with stable IDs and its revision, rather than maintaining competing engineering records.

For an existing `concept.json` or an explicit version-1 workflow, read [the legacy record contract](references/record.md), run `python <skill-dir>/scripts/concept.py check <record>`, and use its `--concept <ID> --review`, `view`, and `diff` modes as appropriate. Do not implicitly convert legacy prose into SysML predicates. Read program findings, repair within the agreed scope, and rerun. Stop repeated unsuccessful repair and surface the unresolved issue. Preserve accepted meanings and declared review scope; changing either is an engineer decision. Use program results for computable claims, without substituting an LLM verdict. Execute scripts without loading their source. Read [provenance](references/notes.md) when explaining or revising the method.

For a separately authored version-2 finite model in this repository, run `python3 scripts/verify.py <model> --review` when typed predicates are explicitly supported. Keep the version-1 concept check for its own record; its qualitative assessments do not become formal predicates automatically. The local verifier is not an engineer acceptance channel or protected runner.

For evidence-based engineering claims, use the [engineering terminology and standards basis](../../docs/engineering-terminology.md). For a requested local prototype, use the [prototype-verification workflow](../../docs/prototype-evaluation.md) when it answers the current question. Keep authored traces, executed observations, verification, validation and acceptance distinct; missing evidence cannot satisfy a selected check. Full ISO conformity is unassessed. Apply draft-shape checks to the requested concept/CONOPS/scenario artifact; a separate verification harness uses compilation/evidence checks without invented concept content.
