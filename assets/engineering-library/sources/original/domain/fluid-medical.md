# Fluid and medical/laboratory iconography: local review candidate

This is a self-contained review set of **64 editable SVGs**: 38 fluid and 26 medical/laboratory icons. It is not an accepted palette revision, an engineering model, an equipment specification, a medical instruction, or a safety label. [index.json](index.json) records each icon's intended concept, treatment, source keys and limits. [icons/](icons/) contains the standalone artwork. Each has a 35 × 35 mm frame, `0 0 160 160` viewBox, accessible title and description, and native vector paths or shapes. The artwork has no visible caption, card, raster image, external link or color-coded meaning. Contact-sheet labels are **outside** the individual art frames and only identify items during review.

The set contains 46 technical illustrations, six local simplified schematics and 12 interface pictograms. Technical illustrations use the [accepted local line specification](../../palette/sections/orthographic-illustrations/line-spec.json): outer contours 2.4, features 1.4 and supporting axes 0.8 local units, with the `8 2 1.5 2` centerline pattern. They are conventionalized views of generic objects, not manufacturer drawings or drawings to scale. Equal frames do not imply equal real sizes. Neither a hidden-line pattern nor numerical dimensions are needed in these views. The miniature centerlines can be faint on a low-resolution display; the printable physical frame is the intended basis for judging detail.

The six physical/schematic pairs are **valve, pump, tank, filter, heat exchanger, and pressure instrument/general instrument**. The six schematics are independent local explanations, not a claimed reproduction of ISA, ISO or any other standard. Piping/process diagrams and hydraulic or pneumatic fluid-power circuits use different contexts and rules; a graphic from this review set is not automatically interchangeable among them. The heat exchanger's two continuous horizontal passages represent two separate paths; their enclosure and internal divider do not connect them. The selected schematic line endpoints are intentional illustrative attachment locations, not evidence of an existing circuit. Arrows on the check-valve cutaway, pump, pressure-loss, and blockage icons indicate an **illustrative** left-to-right direction only; they do not assert a real component's direction, observed flow or measured condition.

The people, instruments and sample objects are independent unless their individual descriptions explicitly show a line between parts of an object. The patient figure is a neutral person whose role comes from context, with no diagnosis, drug, device connection or workflow implied. The blood-pressure cuff connects to its pictured device, not a patient; the infusion line ends freely. Assay, specimen, contamination, sterilization and biosecurity figures convey local concepts only: they are not test results, evidence of contamination, certification of sterilization, hazard warnings, or security controls. A monochrome treatment does not turn them into compliant GHS or ISO safety labels. Provide an appropriate contextual text label when recognition or consequences matter, and apply the project-specific review, labeling and safety requirements separately.

## Reference keys and what they support

All icon geometry in this set was authored independently from generic forms. It was **not traced, copied, or intentionally derived from a figure in ISA-5.1**, any ISO standard, or a manufacturer's drawing. Some independently made schematic shapes may resemble familiar conventions; similarity does not establish origin or conformity. The links below establish scope and usage boundaries, not approval of these particular shapes. Full standards and their figures were not evaluated for conformance.

- `palette` — [Local accepted orthographic illustrations](../../palette/sections/orthographic-illustrations/README.md) and [line specification](../../palette/sections/orthographic-illustrations/line-spec.json). Applied to the frame, line hierarchy and detail budget. These are local design rules, not external standards certification.
- `isa-scope` — [ISA5.1 committee: scope and purpose](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-standards-committees/isa5-1). ISA describes instrumentation and control designation and identification for drawings; this reference was consulted **only for the public scope**, not its protected standard contents or figures. ISA-5.1-specific artwork and conformance remain unverified.
- `check-valve-operation` — [Spirax Sarco, Check Valves](https://www.spiraxsarco.com/learn-about-steam/pipeline-ancillaries/check-valves?sc_lang=en-GB). The public description of a ball resting on an inlet seat and moving downstream under forward pressure informed the independently drawn open-position cutaway; it is not a drawing of the manufacturer’s equipment.
- `iso-fluid-scope` — [ISO 1219-1:2012 public abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/60184.html). The abstract identifies a fluid-power graphical-symbol/circuit context. It does not make these generic process and equipment drawings ISO symbols. Its complete content and amendments were not evaluated.
- `fda-labeling` — [US FDA, Use of Symbols in Labeling FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-labeling/use-symbols-labeling-frequently-asked-questions). Used to distinguish contextual pictograms from device labeling and its applicable symbol rules. This local set is not a labeling review.
- `iso-medical-scope` — [ISO 15223-1:2021 public abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/77326.html?browse=tc). Used only for the scope of manufacturer-supplied medical-device information. No registered or standardized symbol is asserted here.
- `who-equipment` — [WHO priority medical devices and associated technical specifications](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-2019-nCoV-MedDev-TS-O2T.V2), 2020. Used to identify monitor, thermometer and pulse-oximeter equipment categories, not to copy device art or claim performance.
- `who-infusion` — [WHO technical specifications for infusion devices](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/tech-spec-infusion-devices-10072020-final-draft), 2020 final draft. Used to distinguish the pump and delivery line as equipment concepts; these drawings do not specify a setup, drug, flow rate or performance.
- `cdc-biosafety` — [CDC, Biosafety in Microbiological and Biomedical Laboratories, 6th edition](https://www.cdc.gov/labs/bmbl/index.html). Used to preserve the boundary between contextual illustrations and risk-based biosafety practice.
- `cdc-biosecurity` — [CDC, DLS Secure Labs](https://www.cdc.gov/secure-labs/php/about/index.html). Used to distinguish safeguarding biological material from accidental-exposure biosafety; the shield and lock do not demonstrate a security control.

## Rebuild and review

Run from this folder:

```sh
python3 generate.py
python3 render.py
python3 check.py
```

`render.py` uses the locally installed `rsvg-convert` by default; `RSVG_CONVERT` may point to another installation. The independent `check.py` requires Pillow and the configured renderer. It checks the explicit requested inventory and IDs, metadata, editable SVG elements, absence of visible text/raster/references, line weights and patterns, uniqueness, 300 dpi rendered bounds and nonblank ink, selected schematic endpoints, proof presence, and two deliberate rejection fixtures. The checker does not determine visual recognition, actual connectivity, standards conformance or suitability for any intended medical or engineering use. Results and per-icon 300 dpi bounds are in [check-report.json](check-report.json).

The four labeled [vector contact sheets](proofs/) contain exactly 35 mm icon frames on 180 × 184 mm pages; matching PDFs can be printed at 100%. The PNGs render each sheet at 96 and 192 dpi; the 96 dpi frames are approximately 132 × 132 pixels, corresponding to 35 mm at that pixel density. All four 96 dpi sheets were visually inspected after generation, and the amended sheets were reinspected: symbols fit their frames and the reviewed details remain visible at that digital size. This is author self-review, not independent recognition testing. Physical printing, assistive-technology testing, native-editor edit/save/reopen, formal-standard review and user/engineer acceptance were not performed.

A browser rendering attempt failed when the sandbox denied Chromium's required socket operation. The contact sheets and raster checks above were completed with the independently installed `rsvg-convert`; the unused [render.cjs](render.cjs) records the attempted browser route and is not required for rebuilding the proofs.
